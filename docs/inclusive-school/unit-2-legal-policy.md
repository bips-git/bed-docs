## International Declarations, Conventions and Proclamations

International policies act as global pressure, guiding individual countries to change their own laws regarding disability and education. 

**Biwako Millennium Framework (BMF, 1993–2012)**
The BMF was a regional action plan for the Asia-Pacific region. It connected the goals of the first Asian Decade of Disabled Persons (starting in 1993) to a new set of targets ending in 2012, specifically pushing governments to prioritize early intervention and inclusive education.
::: tip Example
Following BMF targets, a government creates a policy requiring all local preschools to screen toddlers for hearing and vision issues so they can receive educational support early.
:::

**The Salamanca Statement and Framework for Action (1994)**
Adopted at a UNESCO conference in Spain, this document changed global thinking. It declared that regular schools must welcome all children regardless of their physical or intellectual state, establishing that inclusive mainstream schools are the most effective way to combat discrimination.
::: tip Example
Instead of sending a child with a learning disability to a separate "special school," a regular neighborhood school adapts its teaching style to include the child, fulfilling the Salamanca ideal.
:::

**UNCRPD (2006)**
The United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (UNCRPD) is a legally binding international treaty. It established that inclusive education is a fundamental human right, shifting the global perspective of disability from a medical charity issue to a human rights issue.
::: tip Example
Disability activists use the UNCRPD to legally challenge a local government that refuses to provide wheelchair ramps in public schools, citing international human rights law.
:::

*Why this matters for a teacher: International declarations provide the moral and legal foundation that empowers teachers to advocate for their students' rights when local systems fail.*

## National Initiatives for Inclusive Education

India has steadily evolved its own policies to bring children with disabilities into mainstream education.

**National Policy on Education (1968 & 1986)**
The 1968 NPE was the first Indian policy to officially recommend integrating children with mild disabilities into regular schools. The 1986 NPE expanded this, stating that moderately disabled children should be in mainstream schools, though it still suggested separate special schools for severely disabled children.
::: tip Example
Under the 1986 NPE guidelines, a student with a mild physical limp attends the local village school, while a student who is completely deaf is sent to a specialized boarding school in the city.
:::

**National Policy on Disability (2006)**
This policy officially recognized persons with disabilities as valuable human resources for the country. It mandated that mainstream schools must become barrier-free (accessible to all) and provide appropriate teaching materials.
::: tip Example
A state government provides funds to regular schools to buy Braille books and build accessible toilets, directly implementing the 2006 policy's barrier-free mandate.
:::

**Right to Education (RTE) Act (2009)**
This historic law made free and compulsory education a legal right for all children aged 6 to 14. Later amendments specifically guaranteed that children with severe or multiple disabilities have the absolute right to learn in a mainstream inclusive environment without facing rejection.
::: tip Example
A private school principal cannot legally deny admission to a 7-year-old child with autism by claiming the school "does not have the facilities," because the RTE Act strictly prohibits such discrimination.
:::

*Why this matters for a teacher: Knowing these laws protects teachers and students, ensuring that inclusive practices are recognized as mandatory legal duties, not optional favors.*

## Institutions for the Education of Children with Disabilities

To turn laws into reality, the Indian government relies on specialized statutory bodies and institutions to train teachers and conduct research.

**Rehabilitation Council of India (RCI)**
Set up as a statutory (legally authorized) body in 1992, the RCI regulates all training programs for special educators and rehabilitation professionals across India. It maintains a Central Rehabilitation Register to license all qualified professionals.
::: tip Example
If a school wants to hire a certified speech therapist or a special educator, the principal will check if the candidate's degree is recognized by the RCI and if they are listed in the official RCI register.
:::

**National Institutes of Different Disabilities**
The government has established specific national institutes for different types of disabilities (such as the National Institute for the Empowerment of Persons with Visual Disabilities, or NIEPVD). These institutes conduct advanced research, design specialized assistive devices, and develop teaching models for specific conditions.
::: tip Example
A mainstream teacher struggling to track the progress of a child with a severe intellectual disability might use a specialized behavioral assessment checklist developed by the National Institute for the Empowerment of Persons with Intellectual Disabilities (NIEPID).
:::

*Why this matters for a teacher: These institutions serve as the ultimate support system, providing teachers with the standardized training and research-backed tools needed to succeed in an inclusive classroom.*

## Key Terms

- **Biwako Millennium Framework (BMF)** — An Asia-Pacific action plan (1993–2012) promoting barrier-free and inclusive societies.
- **Salamanca Statement** — A 1994 UNESCO declaration establishing that regular schools with an inclusive orientation are the most effective way to combat discrimination.
- **UNCRPD** — A 2006 international treaty that legally guarantees persons with disabilities the right to inclusive education.
- **RTE Act (2009)** — An Indian law making free and compulsory education a fundamental right for all children, including those with disabilities.
- **Rehabilitation Council of India (RCI)** — The official Indian government body that regulates and licenses special education teachers and rehabilitation professionals.

## Quick Recap

- International agreements like BMF, Salamanca (1994), and UNCRPD (2006) shifted global perspectives from viewing disability as a medical problem to recognizing inclusive education as a fundamental human right.
- Indian policies progressed from partial integration in the NPE (1968, 1986) to demanding completely barrier-free environments in the National Policy on Disability (2006).
- The RTE Act (2009) legally forces all Indian schools to provide free, compulsory, and inclusive education without discrimination.
- The Rehabilitation Council of India (RCI) ensures the quality of special educators by standardizing their training and licensing.
- National Institutes for different disabilities provide crucial backing by researching new teaching methods and designing assistive technology for specific impairments.
